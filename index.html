Final Course Reflection

Taking 2116CALC-IT with Professor Jonathan Mallari changed my perspective of mathematics in computing. At first calculus was just a theoretical subject. The course gradually made me realize how my prior knowledge of limits, rates of change, and curve optimization is related to computer science and modern web design.

The work on limits and indeterminate forms has shown me that structured problem solving is like debugging. When a denominator is zero, as can happen with rational functions or radical expressions, you cannot simply take a guess. So you need to factor and rationalize the function in order for you to be able to process it. This analytical experience improved my ability to analyze codes and identify logical bugs while writing well-structured routines in JavaScript and CSS.

Another important factor is the way that differential calculus has been able to turn abstract theory into practical applications that are used in the programming of websites. All the concepts of the instantaneous rate of change of f'(x) drive the CSS transition curves of animated user interfaces and determine the speed of movement in front-end systems. The same applications are used to find local extremes, i.e. points where f'(x) = 0, when tuning a system to reduce database query delays, memory allocation, and estimating maximum network speed. Thus, 2116CALC-IT gave me the base of mathematics and proficiency in analysis necessary for becoming a good programmer.
